Problem

A financial brand that didn't look — or feel — trustworthy

For an NBFC, trust is the product. Comfort Fincap's existing website undercut that trust before a visitor even reached the product pages. Everything was crammed into three pages with no clear hierarchy, loan types were buried rather than showcased, and there was nothing on the site that signaled credibility to a first-time visitor comparing lenders.

OLD WEBSITE PROBLEMS

  • Cramped 3-page layout
  • Loan types buried with no distinction between products
  • No credibility proof (no trust signals, no track record)
  • Inconsistent visual branding across touchpoints
  • No clear path for different user types

WHAT WE NEEDED TO BUILD

  • A 10+ page structure with clear service hierarchy
  • Dedicated, scannable pages for each loan/financing product
  • Visible trust markers — track record, process clarity, credibility cues
  • A cohesive, modern green-black design system
  • Segmented journeys for borrowers vs. investors
Key insight from research:

The three root problems broke down almost proportionally — roughly half of user friction traced back to the cluttered layout hiding loan types, about a third to inconsistent branding undermining investor confidence, and the remainder to the complete absence of a clear service hierarchy. Fixing the layout alone wouldn't have been enough; the branding and hierarchy problems had to be solved together, or trust would still leak out somewhere in the journey.

Overview
What is Comfort Fincap?
Comfort Fincap Limited is a Mumbai-based NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company) and part of the Comfort Group, offering Loan Against Securities, Loan Against Property, Supply Chain Financing, Consumer Durable Loans, and Promoter Funding to individuals, MSMEs, and corporates across India. As a regulated lender, its website isn't just a marketing brochure — it's often the first, and sometimes only, point of contact a borrower or investor has with the company before deciding whether to trust it with their money.

I was brought in to redesign the company's website end-to-end: from research and information architecture through to high-fidelity UI and developer handoff. The existing site was a dense 3-page layout that buried the company's loan products, offered no credibility signals, and made it hard for either audience — borrowers looking for capital, or investors evaluating the company — to find what they needed.

User Research

Two audiences, one financial decision

Financial services websites typically serve two very different mindsets under one roof: people who need money, and people deciding whether to trust a company with theirs. Research and competitive analysis focused on understanding both.

BORROWERS

  • MSMEs, business owners, individuals seeking funds against property, securities, or receivables
  • Goal: quickly understand which loan product fits their need and how to apply
  • Pain: couldn't tell Loan Against Securities apart from Loan Against Property or Supply Chain Financing at a glance
  • Need: clear product pages, simple language an obvious next step

INVESTORS

  • Existing and prospective investors evaluating the company's credibility and financial standing
  • Goal: assess trustworthiness, track record, and transparency before engaging
  • Pain: no visible credibility markers — nothing that distinguished Comfort Fincap from any other lender
  • Need: a polished, professional presentation that signals stability and legitimacy

Design Process

Information Architecture — the core fix

The single biggest design decision was breaking the old 3-page structure apart. Instead of stacking every loan product, every proof point, and every CTA onto a handful of dense pages, the new IA gives each product line — Loan Against Securities, Loan Against Property, Supply Chain Financing, Consumer Durable Loans, and Promoter Funding — its own page with a consistent template: what it is, who it's for, how it works, and how to apply. A borrower researching Supply Chain Financing should never have to wade through content meant for an investor, or scroll past unrelated products, to find what they came for.

The redesigned experience

With a financial brand, the visual design has to do quiet, specific work: it needs to read as established and regulated, not flashy or startup-like, while still being easy to navigate on a phone. The redesign centres on a green-black identity system, high-fidelity Figma screens, and an interactive prototype that carries the same visual language across every touchpoint — homepage, product pages, and lead-capture flows.

  • Product clarity.
  • Trust-first structure.
  • Consistent design system.
  • Responsive by default.

Design Decisions

Key decisions and why

Usability testing with real borrowers

I tested internally with stakeholders since timeline didn't allow external recruitment — the fastest way to validate structure early. But stakeholders already understand the loan products, so they can't replicate a first-time borrower's confusion. Real usability testing would have surfaced friction around financial jargon and trust cues that insiders no longer notice.

A/B testing the loan comparison flow

The redesign assumes one path is clearest for choosing between loan types, but I'd A/B test 2-3 layout variants with live users before locking the final structure — trust-heavy decisions deserve data, not instinct alone.

Accessibility audit for a regulated industry

Financial platforms serve a wide range of users, including elderly investors and first-time borrowers. A formal accessibility audit — contrast, font sizing, screen reader support — would make the design compliant, not just visually clean.

Trust-signal analytics dashboard

The redesign was scoped around structure and credibility, but a lightweight analytics layer tracking where investors drop off or hesitate would let Comfort Fincap keep improving trust signals after launch, not just at delivery.

Outcomes

What I would do differently

10+ pages, one per product line

Replaced a cramped 3-page layout with 10+ dedicated pages, giving each loan product and service its own space instead of being squeezed together.

Cluttered layout hiding loan types

50% of user friction traced back to the cluttered layout that buried loan types from view. Resolved by restructuring the information architecture so each product is easy to find on its own.

Inconsistent branding undermining trust

30% of friction traced to inconsistent branding that undermined trust. Resolved with a unified green–black design system applied across the whole site.

One cohesive design system across every flow

For the first time, a single design system was carried consistently across the homepage, product pages, and lead-capture flows — instead of each section looking and feeling different.
